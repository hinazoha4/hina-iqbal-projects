4.2 Homogeneous transformations
It is common to embed a transformation in an nD space in a (n +1 )D one
to linearise or take advantage otherwise. This is what we can do with the
concept of homogeneous coordinates. In fact it simply consists in adding
an additional component to the coordinates of points or vectors. So a
2D point [x, y] becomes [x, y, 1] in homogeneous coordinates. Additionally
any point [εx, εy, ε] represents the same point as the homogenisation of
the coordinates can be done always by multiplying the whole vector by
the scale factor 1/ε. This means that the additional coordinate can never
be zero in this representation, although this restriction does not apply in
projective geometry often used in computer vision.

4.3 Using GLM
To support matrix creation and basic linear algebra operations we can use
the GLM library in C++, PyGLM equivalent for Python, or the glm-js
equivalent for JavaScript.

Important note: GLM, GLSL, and other computing languages such
as Fortran, store matrices in column-wise order. This is the opposite of
what happens in C or C++, where the sequence of elements in memory
correspond to row-after-row. This may be somewhat confusing as the rows
output by mat.toString() are indeed the columns. By consequence, when
you specify the elements of a matrix on its declaration (constructor) you
are supplying them in column order.
