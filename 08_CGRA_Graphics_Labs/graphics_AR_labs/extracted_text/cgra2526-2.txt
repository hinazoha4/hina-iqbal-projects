2D Shapes and Time
Graphics viewed on a computer screen are always made of pixels with dif-
ferent colours (images). Any shape to be displayed always undergoes a
ﬁnal stage where it is converted from coordinates of its vertices (and edges)
to images. As previously said, the shapes are modelled by the relative
positions of their vertices, that in turn are grouped for form polygons (tri-
angles in most cases). A solid is then a set of polygons where the edges are
not drawn but instead the inner region corresponding pixels are ﬁlled with
colours, according to some colour/lighting model. The exercises below are
related with modelling simple shapes and the application of some simple
geometric transformations.
2.1 2D Shapes and Transformations
On the given triangle example notebook you can ﬁnd an example that
draw a triangle whose color varies from red to blue to green. In fact this
is an automatic GL behaviour for interpolating information. Notice that
the corner coordinates are in the range [→1, 1], and as you can see on the
second example when these coordinates are outside this range the result is
a clipped image. Thus, you may retain this rule only vertices with (ﬁnal)
coordinates x,y,z in the [→1, 1] range are going to be drawn . We will come
to this subject later.

2.2 Time
The introduction of time-dependent movements brings always a new dimen-
sion in the attractiveness of computer graphics. Frequently for the purpose
of producing movement, programmers use counters that increase distances,
angles, or other parameters. Despite that, the achieved results depend on
how fast the computer is, or how heavy are the remaining computations.
Thus, and depending on the computer speed, the increments may be done
faster or slower and consequently the same happens to the afected move-
ments (or evolutions).
To avoid this, it is important to use real time measures from the under-
lying operating system via some function available. Consider the use of the
following pseudocode
