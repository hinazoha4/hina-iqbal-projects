A cone is modeled by a set of vertices uniformly distributed along a
base circle, where consecutive vertex pairs connect to a single apex
vertex to create triangular lateral faces.
A cylinder is constructed from two parallel circles where consecutive
pairs of base and top vertices form a quad face, tessellated into two
triangles.
A sphere is modeled as a stack of circular cross-sections whose radius
varies with the cosine of the elevation angle. Adjacent circular rings
are linked into triangular strips similar to cylinders, with the polar
caps degenerating into cones at radius 0.

A.3 Compound Shapes
By combining the basic primitives above through local coordinate trans-
formations, you can create hierarchical compound objects. For instance,
a siege engine might combine scaled cubes for chassis beams, cylinders for
axles and rollers, and cones or discs for structural ﬁttings. Transformations
applied to the root entity must cascade down to all constituent child parts.
